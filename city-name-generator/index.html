    About this list

    How this city name generator works

    Real place names are usually built from a small set of recurring pieces: a directional or descriptive prefix (North, Port, Lake, Fort), a root word describing the landscape (-field, -brook, -ridge, -haven), and a settlement suffix (-ton, -ville, -burg, -shire). This generator recombines those same pieces at random, alongside a handful of fully standalone invented names, so every result reads as a place that could plausibly exist without being a copy of a real one.

    Naming advice

    How to build a believable fictional setting

    Let geography inform the name

    A coastal city, a mountain town, and a river crossing each tend to carry different naming clues in the real world (port, peak, ford). Picking a generated name that matches your setting's actual geography makes the world feel more thought-through.

    Keep a consistent naming convention across your map

    If several cities in your story share a region or culture, try to keep their naming style consistent — all drawing from similar prefixes and suffixes — the same way real regions often share naming conventions (many English towns end in -ton or -ham, for instance).

    Reserve unique, standalone names for your most important locations

    Your capital city or main setting can afford a more distinctive, one-of-a-kind name, while smaller background towns can lean on more generic prefix-and-suffix combinations — this mirrors how real-world naming tends to work too.

    Say it out loud and check it's easy to use in dialogue

    A city name that's awkward to say will get in the way every time a character mentions it. Read your shortlist aloud as if a character were casually referencing "back home in ___."

    Double-check it doesn't already belong to a real, well-known place

    Since the generator recombines common real naming pieces, it's worth a quick search on your favorite result to make sure it isn't already a well-known real city, which could be confusing for readers.

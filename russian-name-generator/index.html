    Country naming notes

    Russian patronymics and formal name structure

    A traditional Russian personal name can contain a given name, a patronymic and a family name. The patronymic is formed from a parent's given name and commonly has a masculine form ending in -ovich or -evich and a feminine form ending in -ovna or -evna. Some names follow special patterns, so a generated example should be checked before it is used in a formal or historical context. The “With Patronymic” option adds a sample patronymic between the given name and surname. It is an illustrative combination, not a record of a real person's family or parentage. In everyday conversation, people may use a given name and a familiar form rather than the full formal sequence.

    Research notes and references

    Russian Full-Name Order, Gendered Forms and Patronymics

    A traditional Russian full name commonly places the given name first, a patronymic second and the family name last. The patronymic is derived from a parent’s given name. Common masculine endings include -ovich and -evich; common feminine endings include -ovna and -evna. These are patterns, not a universal rule for every name root. The generator’s “With Patronymic” option adds a sample from a curated list, but it does not infer a real parent’s name or create a real family record.

    Many Russian surnames also have masculine and feminine forms, often changing -ov to -ova, -ev to -eva or -in to -ina. The generator uses these common patterns for its feminine surname bank. Some surnames follow different rules, and transliteration from Cyrillic into Latin letters can vary between systems. When a story needs precise language or period accuracy, verify the original Cyrillic spelling and the exact form.

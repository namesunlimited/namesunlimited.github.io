    About this list

    What are elf names like in fantasy?

    Elf names in fantasy usually have a flowing, multi-syllable first name, often paired with a surname or house name drawn from nature, light or the stars. This generator follows that convention: it pairs an elvish-sounding first name with an elvish-style surname such as Moonshadow or Silverleaf, and you can choose male, female or mixed names, full names, first names only or surnames only.

    How does the elf name generator work?

    It picks at random from built-in lists (30 male first names, 30 female first names and 20 surnames) and combines them.     Elf name ideas by sub-type

    Fantasy settings often split elves into sub-types with slightly different naming conventions. Here is how to read the results for each style.

    High elf names

    Longer, more formal-sounding names suit the classic high elf archetype. Look for results like Ithrandor Silverleaf or Miraval Starweaver.

    Wood elf names

    Shorter, earthier combinations with nature-themed surnames suit wood elves, such as Faelar Thornwood or Nimriel Ashgrove.

    Dark elf and drow-style names

    Sharper, harder-sounding combinations work well: favour shorter first names paired with a harder surname like Shadowmere or Wraithwind. For a dedicated list, the fantasy name generator has a Dark Elf (Drow) option.

    Half-elf names

    For a character bridging two cultures, pair an elvish first name with a plainer human surname from the character name generator to suggest mixed heritage.

    Naming advice

    How do you choose a good elf name?

    Choose a name that fits the character's role, sounds consistent with the rest of their people, and is easy enough to say aloud. A few checks make the choice faster.

    Match the weight of the name to the role

    A noble ruler reads better with a longer, formal name. A scout or ranger often suits something shorter and sharper. Generate a larger batch and sort the results loosely by weight before you decide.

    Keep one naming convention per clan or region

    If several elf characters belong to the same house or region, reuse the same surname or pick first names with a similar rhythm so they read as one culture.

    Say it out loud before committing

    Soft elvish-style names can still trip the tongue. Read your shortlist aloud, especially if the name will be spoken in dialogue, in a podcast or voiced in a game.

    Decide whether you need a full name

    Many stories only use a first name in dialogue. If the surname will never appear on the page, choose First Only and save the house name for characters who need one.

    Check for collisions with famous characters

    Elf names are strongly associated with a few major franchises, so search a name you like before you commit to it, particularly for published work.

    Frequently asked questions

    What are good elf names for a D&amp;D character?

    Pick a name that matches the elf sub-type and the character's role. Longer, formal names suit nobles and high elves, while shorter names with a nature surname suit wood elf rangers. Generate a batch, shortlist a few and say them aloud at the pace you would use at the table.

    How do you make a name sound elvish?

    Use flowing vowels and soft consonants (l, r, s, th), favour two to four syllables, and avoid hard stops such as k or g. Pairing the first name with a surname based on light, nature or stars reinforces the style.

    Are the elf names based on a specific fantasy series?

    They are written in a general high-fantasy elvish style rather than copied from a single series. A few entries resemble names from published fantasy, so check any name you plan to publish.

    Do the surnames work for a whole elven clan?

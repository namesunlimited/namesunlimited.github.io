    Name guide

    Chinese family names and given names

    Chinese names are commonly written with the family name first, followed by the given name. Many family names are one syllable in Mandarin romanisation, while given names may contain one or two syllables. The examples here use pinyin-style Latin spellings without tone marks.

    Country naming notes

    Chinese characters, Pinyin and name order

    Chinese names are usually presented with the family name first, followed by the given name. Many family names use one character, while given names commonly use one or two characters. The generator displays Hanzi first and puts a readable Pinyin form in parentheses, for example 王伟 (Wang Wei). Pinyin is a romanisation system, and tone marks are omitted here to keep the output easy to type and search. A character can have several readings or meanings depending on context, and the same Pinyin spelling may correspond to different characters. Do not infer a person's identity or a name's meaning from romanisation alone.

    Research notes and references

    Chinese Name Order, Hanzi, Pinyin and Character Choices

    Chinese names are commonly written with the family name first and the given name after it. This generator displays Hanzi characters with Pinyin in parentheses, and the complete-name format keeps the surname before the given name. The Pinyin shown is a practical reading guide; the Hanzi is the actual selected character form. Names that share a Pinyin spelling can use different characters and have different meanings, so a romanised syllable by itself does not establish the correct Hanzi.

    The 2020 national name report published by Xinhua from the Ministry of Public Security’s household-registration research centre lists 王 (Wang), 李 (Li), 张 (Zhang), 刘 (Liu) and 陈 (Chen) as the five most common family names in the registered population at that time. Those five surnames accounted for 30.8% of the registered population. The generator includes many more examples, but the tail of a 100-name bank is not presented as equally frequent. Its selected Hanzi and Pinyin pairs are illustrative; confirm a person’s intended characters with a fluent speaker or a reliable name dictionary.

    Where these Chinese name lists come from

    Reviewed: 12 November 2026. Common surname choices and example given names were checked against the 2020 national name report. The displayed Latin spelling follows the Chinese personal-name Pinyin spelling standard GB/T 28039-2011, which remains current after its September 2025 review.     Common questions

    Chinese name generator FAQs

    Does this Chinese name generator put the surname first?

    Yes. Full-name results place the family name before the given name, following a common Chinese name order.

    Does the generator show Chinese characters?

    Yes. Results display selected Hanzi characters with Pinyin in parentheses. Pinyin can map to different characters, so treat each Hanzi and Pinyin pair as an illustrative selection and verify it when exact meaning matters.

    Can I generate Chinese male and female names?

    Yes. Choose male, female or any gender before generating given names or full-name combinations.

    Are the generated names guaranteed to be culturally or linguistically perfect?

    No. They are brainstorming examples. For a real person's name or a character requiring precise cultural detail, verify the characters, tones and meaning with a fluent speaker.

    Why does the Chinese generator show Hanzi and Pinyin together?

    Hanzi preserves the Chinese written characters, while Pinyin gives an accessible Latin-letter reading. The Pinyin here omits tone marks and is intended as a practical guide.

    Why does the family name appear first?

    Chinese names are commonly written family name first, followed by the given name. The generator follows that order in complete-name results.

    Do all Chinese given names have one fixed meaning?

    No. Meaning depends on the selected characters and context. Different characters can share the same Pinyin, so do not assign a meaning from romanised spelling alone.

    Are the masculine and feminine lists strict gender rules?

    No. They are sample groupings for browsing convenience. Chinese given-name usage varies, and some names can be used by people of different genders.

    Does one Pinyin spelling always map to one Hanzi character?

    No. A Pinyin syllable can correspond to different characters. The generator shows selected illustrative Hanzi and Pinyin pairs, not every possible character choice.

    Can I use the generated Hanzi as a verified person’s name?

    Treat it as a draft only. Confirm characters, tones, intended meaning and cultural fit with a fluent speaker or reliable Chinese-language reference before using a name for a real person.

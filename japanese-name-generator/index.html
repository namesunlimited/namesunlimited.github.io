How do Japanese names work?

A Japanese name has a family name followed by a given name, and it is usually written in kanji. The generator shows both orders: the kanji line puts the family name first, the way a name is written in Japan, while the romaji line puts the given name first, the way it is often written in English.

Why does the same Japanese name have different meanings?

The meaning of a Japanese name depends on the kanji used, not only on how it sounds. The same pronunciation can be written with different characters, and parents often choose the characters for both sound and meaning. This generator lists Aoi twice for that reason: 葵 refers to a hollyhock flower, while 碧 refers to a blue-green colour.

A short English phrase can only summarise a name. Treat the meaning shown on each card as one common reading of its kanji, not the single correct translation.

How do kanji readings affect a name?

One kanji can be read several ways, and names sometimes use unusual readings. That is why a name written in kanji cannot always be read correctly by guessing, and why Japanese forms often ask for a name in kana (the phonetic script) as well.

How do you choose a Japanese name for a character?

Used as an anime name generator, the tool gives you names that sound natural for a Japanese setting. It also works for manga, games and role-play characters. Pick a name that fits the character's age, setting and personality, then check that the kanji combination makes sense. Some practical points:

  • Match the era. A name that is common for children today may feel out of place in a story set a century ago.
  • Choose meaning on purpose. Because meaning depends on kanji, you can pick characters that reflect personality, such as strength, light or the seasons.
  • Mind the order. Decide whether your story gives names in Japanese order or Western order, and stay consistent.
  • Check important names. For published work, ask someone who reads Japanese or use a kanji dictionary to confirm readings and meanings.

Looking for an Asia name generator?

This page is a Japanese name generator, not a general Asia name generator. It makes Japanese names only, and it does not cover Chinese, Korean, Indian or other Asian naming traditions. Asia has many languages, and each one has its own characters, sounds and naming rules, so a single list would not fit them all.

Some traditions look similar at first. Chinese and Korean names, for example, also usually put the family name first. The names and their meanings are still different, so do not swap them for Japanese names in a story that needs to feel accurate.

How do Japanese names work?

Is the family name or the given name first in Japanese?

What does this Japanese name generator do?

It creates Japanese-style full names and shows each one in kanji and in romaji, with a short meaning for the given name. Choose male, female or unisex given names and a batch of 12, 24 or 48 names, then click a card to copy the romaji, the kanji and the meaning together.

The names come from a small built-in list (12 male, 12 female and 6 unisex given names, and 12 common surnames) that is recombined at random. It is a source of ideas for characters, anime and manga projects, and stories, not a complete or official naming database.

How do Japanese names work?

A Japanese name has a family name followed by a given name, and it is usually written in kanji. The generator shows both orders: the kanji line puts the family name first, the way a name is written in Japan, while the romaji line puts the given name first, the way it is often written in English.

Why does the same name have different meanings?

The meaning of a Japanese name depends on the kanji used, not only on how it sounds. The same pronunciation can be written with different characters, and parents often choose the characters for both sound and meaning. This generator lists Aoi twice for that reason: 葵 refers to a hollyhock-type flower, while 碧 refers to a blue-green colour.

A short English phrase can only summarise a name. Treat the meaning shown on each card as one common reading of its kanji, not the single correct translation.

How do kanji readings affect a name?

One kanji can be read several ways, and names sometimes use unusual readings. That is why a name written in kanji cannot always be read correctly by guessing, and why Japanese forms often ask for a name in kana (the phonetic script) as well.

How do you choose a Japanese name for a character?

Used as an anime name generator, the tool gives you names that sound natural for a Japanese setting. Pick a name that fits the character's age, setting and personality, then check that the kanji combination makes sense. Some practical points:

  • Match the era. A name that is common for children today may feel out of place in a story set a century ago.
  • Choose meaning on purpose. Because meaning depends on kanji, you can pick characters that reflect personality, such as strength, light or the seasons.
  • Mind the order. Decide whether your story gives names in Japanese order or Western order, and stay consistent.
  • Check important names. For published work, ask someone who reads Japanese or use a kanji dictionary to confirm readings and meanings.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do Japanese names work?

A Japanese name is written with the family name first and the given name second, normally in kanji. The meaning comes from the kanji chosen, and the same sound can be written with different kanji.

Do Japanese names have one fixed meaning?

No. The meaning depends on the kanji used. A short English phrase is only a simplification, so the same pronunciation can carry different meanings in different families.

Is the family name or the given name first?

In Japan the family name comes first. In English-language writing the order is often reversed, so this generator shows the kanji with the family name first and the romaji with the given name first.

Are these names suitable for anime and manga characters?

Yes. They are real-sounding Japanese names that suit anime, manga, games and fiction. They are selected examples, so check readings and meanings if accuracy matters for published work.

Can I copy both the romaji and the kanji?

Yes. Click a card or its Copy button to copy the romaji name, the kanji and the meaning together, or use Copy All for the whole batch.

What do the kanji in names represent?

Kanji are characters that originated in Chinese writing. In names they often stand for nature, seasons, virtues or hopes for the child.

    About this list

    How this Japanese name generator works

    This tool pairs a genuine Japanese first name with a genuine Japanese surname, so every result is a plausible, natural-sounding full name rather than an invented one. Names are romanized (written in the Latin alphabet) since the same sound can usually be written with several different kanji, each carrying its own meaning — pick "First Only" or "Last Only" if you just need one half of the name.

    Japanese surnames are far more concentrated than in many other languages — a relatively small number of family names, including Sato, Suzuki, Takahashi, Tanaka, and Watanabe, are shared by a large share of the population. First names, on the other hand, are much more varied and often carry a specific meaning tied to the kanji chosen for them, which is part of why Japanese naming conventions place heavy weight on meaning rather than sound alone.

    Naming advice

    How to choose a Japanese name

    Decide if you need a full name or just one part

    A character or username often only needs a first name; a baby name or a full profile usually needs both. Deciding this first narrows the tool's settings and avoids generating a surname you'll just have to discard.

    Think about the meaning, not just the sound

    Japanese naming culture leans heavily on meaning — many parents choose a name specifically for what its kanji represents (nature, virtue, strength, beauty) rather than purely for how it sounds. If a name's meaning matters for your project, look up a few kanji options for the romanized name you land on.

    Check how the name reads for non-Japanese speakers, if relevant

    If your project has an international audience — a game, a novel, a brand — it's worth checking the name isn't awkward to pronounce or doesn't unintentionally resemble an unrelated word in your primary audience's language.

    Match formality to context

    Some Japanese first names read as more traditional and formal, others as softer or more modern — similar to how "Robert" and "Kai" carry different tones in English. Generate a larger batch and compare a few side by side to find the right register for your character or project.

    Pair consistently if naming multiple characters

    If you're naming a full cast for a story or game, keep the naming convention consistent — mixing heavily traditional names with very modern ones for characters from the same setting can read as inconsistent unless that contrast is intentional.

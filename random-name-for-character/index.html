What is a character name generator?

A character name generator gives you ready-made first and last names for the people in a story. Pick a genre (modern, sci-fi, fantasy, Victorian, villain or superhero) and a gender, choose a batch of 12, 24 or 48, and click a name to copy it.

Each result combines a first name and a surname from short lists written for that genre, so the same names will come up again across batches. Use the results as starting points: keep the first name you like and change the surname, or adjust a spelling until the name feels right for your character.

How do you choose a good character name?

Choose a name that fits the character's personality, role, setting and genre, and that readers can say and remember easily. Work through these questions:

  • Who is the character? A name can hint at age, background or attitude. Decide what you want it to suggest.
  • Where and when is the story set? A Victorian novel and a near-future thriller call for very different names.
  • How does it sound on the page? Short, punchy names such as Jack Cross tend to read as brisk and direct, while longer, softer names such as Genevieve Bellamy often read as formal or refined. These are tendencies, not rules.
  • Can readers say it? A name your readers cannot pronounce is easy to forget. Keep it simple unless the strangeness is the point.

How do character names change by genre?

Each genre has its own conventions, and the generator's lists follow them loosely.

  • Modern fiction and thrillers: believable everyday names, often with a sharp surname.
  • Sci-fi and cyberpunk: shorter, invented-sounding or technical names such as Vex or Kestrel.
  • High fantasy: older or romantic names with a place or nature surname. For race-based fantasy names, use the fantasy name generator or the elf name generator.
  • Victorian and historical: period names such as Beatrice or Archibald with traditional surnames like Hawthorne.
  • Villains and antiheroes: use the Dark Villains genre as a villain name generator for darker or sharper sounds, though an ordinary-sounding name can be more unsettling than an obvious one.
  • Superheroes: the Superheroes genre works as a superhero name generator, and names work more like code names, for example Apex Justice. Add a civilian name separately if your character needs one.

How should protagonist and villain names differ?

There is no rule, but protagonist names usually need to be easy to read hundreds of times without friction, while villain names are free to be more distinctive. Sharp sounds can signal menace, though avoid cliché names such as the obvious "dark" words if you want the villain to feel real. Whatever you choose, make sure the two names look and sound different on the page.

How do you avoid similar character names?

Give major characters different first letters, different lengths and different endings. Names like John and Josh, or Mara and Mira, are easy to confuse in a long story. List your cast on one page and check that no two names look alike at a glance.

Can you use generated names directly?

You can, but a quick check is worth it. Some names, such as Bellatrix, Morgana or Lilith, are strongly tied to existing characters. Search the final name before publishing, and combine or alter parts if it points to someone famous. For a longer guide, see how to choose a name for a fictional character. Frequently Asked Questions

How do you choose a good character name?

Match the name to the character's personality, role, setting and genre, say it out loud, and check that it is distinct from other names in your story. A good name is easy to read and fits the world it appears in.

What makes a character name memorable?

Distinct sounds, a clear rhythm and a link to the character's role make a name stick. Names that start with a different letter from the rest of your cast are easier to tell apart.

Should fictional names be easy to pronounce?

Usually yes, because readers skip names they cannot say. Unusual names work when the story gives readers a reason and a way to pronounce them.

Are generated character names copyrighted?

Names on their own are generally not protected by copyright, but a name tied to a famous character or brand can still cause trademark or confusion problems. This is not legal advice, so search any name you plan to publish.

Can I use these names for villains and superheroes?

Yes. Choose the Dark Villains or Superheroes genre for names written in those styles. Superhero results work like code names.

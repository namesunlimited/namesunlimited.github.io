    By platform

    Username styles for different platforms

    The right username style depends on where you're using it — a handle that reads well on a professional network can feel stiff on a gaming platform, and vice versa.

    Social media usernames

    Social handles get read constantly, in profile headers, comment sections, and shared links, so clarity matters. A clean firstname.lastname or firstname_lastname pattern is easy to say out loud and easy to search for.

    Gaming usernames and gamertags

    Gaming culture tolerates — and often rewards — more personality: shorter, punchier handles that hint at playstyle or interests tend to stand out more than a plain name-plus-number combination.

    Professional and work-related usernames

    For LinkedIn, a work Slack, or a professional portfolio, a straightforward firstname.lastname format is usually the safest choice — it's easy for colleagues and recruiters to find and remember.

    Anonymous or privacy-focused usernames

    If the goal is not being easily identified, avoid combinations that include your real birth year, hometown, or full legal name — a generated handle built from a different first and last name than your own works well here.

    Naming advice

    How to build a username that lasts

    Don't reuse the same username and password pattern

    A username you reuse everywhere — combined with a weak or reused password — gives anyone who gets hold of one leaked credential a head start guessing your accounts elsewhere. It's worth treating your username choice with almost as much care as your password strategy.

    Aim for the 6–15 character sweet spot

    Very short usernames get claimed fast and are often unavailable; very long ones are awkward to type and easy to mistype when someone's searching for you. Something in the middle tends to balance memorability with availability.

    Add a real modifier instead of a random number

    If your first choice is taken, tacking on a random number can look like an afterthought. Adding a word that means something — a role, a hobby, an action verb — tends to look more intentional and is easier to remember than "name4821."

    Check availability before you commit

    Search the exact handle you want across the platforms you'll actually use, and ideally check it isn't already strongly associated with someone else, to avoid impersonation confusion.

    Keep it consistent once you've chosen

    Using the same (or a close variant of the same) handle across platforms makes you easier to find and builds recognition over time, rather than starting from zero on every new site.

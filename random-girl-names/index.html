    Naming advice

    How to choose the right girl name

    Start with a style, not a shortlist

    Rather than jumping straight to individual names, decide whether you're drawn to soft and feminine, vintage, nature-inspired, or bold and modern. Once the style feels right, individual names get much easier to compare.

    Test the full name out loud

    Say the first name, middle name, and surname together. What reads well on paper can sound clumsy out loud, especially if the syllables or ending sounds clash with your last name.

    Picture the nicknames it invites

    Elizabeth becomes Liz, Eliza, or Beth; Charlotte becomes Lottie or Charlie. If a nickname feels inevitable, make sure you'd be happy calling your daughter that every day.

    Check initials and monograms

    Write out the first, middle, and last initials together and make sure they don't spell out anything unfortunate on a monogrammed gift or a school desk.

    Weigh popularity against individuality

    A popular name means she'll likely share it with classmates; a rare name makes her stand out but may draw more spelling and pronunciation questions. There's no wrong answer — just a preference worth talking through together.

    Look into the name's meaning and origin

    A name's backstory — where it comes from, what it traditionally means — can turn a name you like into one you're certain about, or reveal a connection you didn't expect.

What are gender neutral names?

Gender neutral names are names that can be given to a boy, a girl or a child of any gender. You may also see them called unisex names. Some, like Jordan and Taylor, have been used for both boys and girls for many years. Others, like Nova and Wren, are newer and feel fresh to most people.

Why are gender neutral names so popular?

More parents want a name that does not box a child in. A neutral name lets a child grow up and decide who they are without a name that points one way. Many parents also simply like how these names sound. They tend to be short, soft and easy to say.

What is the difference between unisex and gender neutral names?

In everyday use, there is no real difference. Unisex names are used for both boys and girls. Gender neutral is a wider phrase that also fits children who do not identify as a boy or a girl. Most lists, including this one, use both terms for the same names.

Can a name be neutral in one country and not in another?

Yes. A name can feel neutral in one place and clearly male or female in another. For example, Andrea is a boy name in Italy and a girl name in the United States. Always check how a name is used where you live and where your family comes from.

Riley, Avery, Charlie, Quinn and River are among the names parents choose most often in the United States. Popularity moves from year to year, so look at your local birth name lists if you want current numbers.

Are nicknames a good way to get a neutral name?

Yes. Many parents pick a longer name and use a neutral short form. Alexander and Alexandra both shorten to Alex. Theodore and Theodora can both be Teddy. Francis and Frances can both be Frankie. This lets a child choose later which version they prefer.

How do you choose a gender neutral name?

Use these simple steps to narrow down your list.

  1. Start with sound. Pick names you love to say out loud.
  2. Say it with your surname. Read the full name a few times. It should feel smooth.
  3. Check nicknames and initials. Think about what the name might be shortened to and what the initials spell.
  4. Look at the meaning. Meanings can make a name feel special, but they are only one part of the choice.
  5. Think about spelling. Simple spellings save a child from explaining their name for life.
  6. Take a few days. Keep a short list and see which name still feels right.

Should a gender neutral name have a middle name?

A middle name is a good place to add a name that points to family or culture. It can also balance the first name. For example, a neutral first name like Quinn goes well with a traditional middle name from your family.

Will a gender neutral name cause problems for my child?

Most children with these names have no trouble at all. Forms and school lists may sometimes need a note, but these names are now very common. The most important thing is that your child likes their name as they grow up.

More questions parents ask about gender neutral names

Can a boy and a girl have the same name?

Yes. Names like Jordan, Taylor, Morgan and Casey are used for both boys and girls, and there is no rule against it.

Do gender neutral names change over time?

They do. A name can start as a boy name and slowly become more common for girls. Avery and Riley are good examples. This is why it is worth checking how a name is used today.

What gender neutral names are good for twins?

Matching styles work well. You could pick two nature names like River and Wren, or two classic names like Jordan and Taylor. Names that sound alike can confuse people, so avoid pairs with the same first letter and ending.

What are good gender neutral names that start with A?

Try Avery, Ariel, Arya, Ash, Atlas, Arlo, Amrit and Alex.

What are good gender neutral names that start with R?

Try Riley, River, Rowan, Reese, Remy, Robin, Rory, Reagan and Rain.

What are good gender neutral names that start with J?

Try Jordan, Jamie, Jesse, Jules, Jay, Justice and Jyoti.

Are gender neutral names a good idea for a baby you do not know the sex of yet?

They can be very helpful. If you are waiting for the birth to find out, a neutral name means you only need to choose once. You can keep the list short and ready for any outcome.

Are neutral names more common for girls or boys?

Many names that began as boy names are now used more often for girls. It is less common to see girl names move toward boys. This does not mean a name belongs to one group. It only shows how usage shifts.

How can I be sure the name is not too common?

Look up the name in your country's birth name records for the past few years. If it ranks in the top 50, many children in your child's class may share it. If you want something less common, pick a name from the modern or nature lists above.
